# Growth & Marketing — practice worksheet

Version: `op-04-practice-2026-10-25`

The people and data in this exercise are fictional.

## What you will practise

Plan a small test to find out which message could encourage people to try a fictional programme.

## Who this is for and what you need

People interested in marketing, communication or finding ways to reach new participants. You should be able to choose an audience, explain what you want to test and decide what result to measure.

### Fictional offer and channel snapshot
Offer: a self-guided introductory workshop with a sample exercise. It provides no job, qualification or guaranteed personal feedback.
Audience A: students who have never used Git. Audience B: working professionals exploring AI-assisted processes.

| Channel | Visits | Example starts |
|---|---:|---:|
| University newsletter | 80 | 16 |
| Professional community | 60 | 18 |

All figures are fictional. The audiences and channels differ, so these rates are not a controlled test. Create a proposed experiment; do not send outreach or buy advertising.


## Tools you can use

- Use a document or spreadsheet.
- AI can help write message options. Check every factual claim yourself.

## Steps to follow

- Choose an audience and explain what you want to find out.
- Write two messages with one clear difference. Explain what you would measure to compare them.
- Describe what results would make you continue, change or stop the test. Explain what a small sample cannot tell you.

## What to include

- Explain your audience choice and test idea, and include both messages.
- Include a measurement table. For each percentage, state what you divide by, and explain how you would decide what to do next.
- List your assumptions and explain how you checked the messages for incorrect or unsupported claims.

## Check your work

- Will the results you measure help answer the question you are testing?
- Do the messages accurately describe what the fictional programme offers?

## Questions to help you improve

- Is the test clear, and does it address a useful question about reaching participants?
- Label your targets as suggestions and your sample results as fictional. Do not present them as agreed targets or real campaign results.
